Master Mental Addition With the Compensation Strategy

## What Is the Compensation Strategy?

Mental math can sometimes feel overwhelming for young learners when they try to perform traditional column addition inside their heads. Re-grouping numbers and carrying over digits mentally requires significant cognitive effort. Fortunately, smart shortcuts exist to make math intuitive and stress-free.

One of the most powerful techniques for upper elementary students is the compensation strategy. This strategy works by rounding one of the numbers in an addition problem to the nearest friendly number (such as a multiple of 10 or 100), performing the quick calculation, and then adjusting or compensating the final answer to account for the change.

By converting tricky numbers into friendly landmarks, students build strong numerical fluency and gain confidence in their problem solving skills.

## Step by Step Worked Examples

Let us look at how compensation works in practice through two clear examples.

### Example 1: Two Digit Addition (48 + 35)

In this problem, 48 is very close to 50, which is much easier to add mentally.

1. Round up to the nearest ten: Add 2 to 48 to make it 50. 2. Add the modified number: Calculate 50 + 35 = 85. 3. Compensate for the addition: Since you added 2 at the beginning to reach 50, you must now subtract 2 from your intermediate sum. 4. Calculate final answer: 85 - 2 = 83.

Therefore, 48 + 35 = 83. With a bit of practice, students can execute these four quick steps in seconds without needing pencil and paper.

### Example 2: Three Digit Addition (198 + 465)

The compensation strategy becomes even more valuable when working with larger numbers.

1. Identify the friendly landmark: 198 is only 2 away from 200. 2. Adjust the first addend: Add 2 to 198 to get 200. 3. Solve the simplified equation: 200 + 465 = 665. 4. Compensate: Subtract the extra 2 that was added earlier. 5. Final calculation: 665 - 2 = 663.

Thus, 198 + 465 = 663. What looked like a daunting three digit addition problem becomes simple mental arithmetic.
